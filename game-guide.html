A quick guide to picking and playing

A few practical pointers for the games in our collection

Not sure what to play? Start with the kind of break you want. If you have a minute or two, an arcade game is easy to dip into. If you want to think through a problem, try a puzzle. If you would rather check in now and then than react quickly, an idle game may suit you. The category labels on the All Games page are a useful starting point, but they are only a guide: games in the same category can feel very different.

Quick games for a short break

Slope is a fast reaction game built around steering a ball along a track. Look a little farther ahead than the next obstacle and make small corrections instead of sharp, last-second turns. The speed makes it tempting to stare at the ball, but watching the space in front of it gives you more time to react.

Drive Mad rewards balance more than constant acceleration. On an unfamiliar obstacle, try easing off the controls and see how the vehicle responds before committing to a jump. Landings matter: a fast approach can get you across a gap, but an awkward angle can leave you upside down just after it.

Moto X3M mixes riding with timed obstacle courses. Learn the rhythm of a section before trying to shave time off it. If the level gives you room to perform a flip, use the airtime carefully; a trick is no help if it leaves too little time to land safely.

When you want a puzzle

In 2048, matching numbered tiles combine when they meet. A useful habit is to keep your largest tile near one corner and avoid moving it away without a reason. Before a move, check what it does to the other rows and columns too; a move that makes one merge can fill the space you needed elsewhere. There is no single pattern that saves every board, so slow down when several directions look equally tempting.

Bloxorz asks you to guide a block across a narrow board and into its goal. Think about the block's position and orientation after each move, not just the square it reaches. When a route looks tight, pause and trace a few moves ahead. If you fall, use the last section as information: notice which turn or orientation made the route fail, then try changing that part rather than repeating the whole sequence unchanged.

Games that reward patience

Cookie Clicker begins with clicking, then adds upgrades and production that can keep making cookies for you. Compare the price of an upgrade with what it adds before spending. Early on, a purchase that noticeably improves production can help more than saving for the biggest item on the screen. Later, the numbers grow quickly, so the upgrade descriptions are more useful than trying to keep every total in your head.

Retro Bowl combines football play with decisions about a team. On offense, taking a moment to read the field before throwing can prevent avoidable interceptions. Between games, consider the roster as a whole rather than spending every resource on one position. A strong player can help, but a team also needs enough depth to cope when the season gets difficult.

Pool and turn-based play

In 8 Ball Pool, a shot is about more than sinking the ball in front of you. Think about where the cue ball will stop and whether it leaves a reasonable next shot. When the game offers aim or power controls, use a lighter touch for close shots and avoid adding force just because it is available. The game's own instructions explain the rules for the version being served here.

Controls and loading tips

Game controls are not all the same. Many desktop games show their keys on a title or instruction screen; others reveal them when you pause. On a phone or tablet, look for on-screen buttons. Touch support and keyboard support depend on the individual game, so a game that works well with a keyboard may not feel as comfortable on a small touchscreen.

Games load inside the player on this site. Give a new game a moment to start, and make sure your browser allows the page to run scripts. If the game stays blank, try reloading once or opening it in a current browser with a stable connection. Fullscreen can give a game more room, but it does not change the game's controls. Some games and their content come from third-party providers; their features and availability can change independently of this site.
